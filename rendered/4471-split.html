The tickets

  1. parser: parser reads day-first dates month first. The shared parser reads every date month first, and every tenant in #4471 writes the day first. Day and month swap in the app, in email and in booking-v2 forms. It covers the parser row above.
  2. hint: booking-v3 form hints MM/DD/YYYY but reads the date day first. booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint says, and the form reads it day first. This happens only through the form, only at booking-v3 tenants, and a parser fix does not touch it. It covers the hint row above, which had no ticket of its own.
  3. one-day move: bookings land one day early, not month-flipped. The booking lands one day early instead of swapping day and month. One invite arrived before the booking was made, and one meeting showed as Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto. This looks like a time-zone bug. It covers the reports in the note under the table, which are filed under parser by tenant.

How the count is made

  • One id, one report: the export pages overlap, so duplicate rows are dropped first (uniqueById).
  • A report’s cause follows its tenant’s locale and booking form (causes.ts), not the tag intake guessed from the reporter’s browser. The “tagged as” column shows where the two disagree.
  • Reports the desk read as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) stay counted where they are filed, and the third ticket lists them again.

Why they were merged

Decision 0001 treats reports as one issue when their subject lines match, and nobody reads the body. The same subjects come in for all three bugs: “Date is wrong” and “Wrong day on the invite” each appear under parser, hint and the one-day move. The subject cannot tell the bugs apart. Tenant and channel can, as the cause check shows. Changing the rule needs a new decision record, because 0001 stays as it was accepted.
